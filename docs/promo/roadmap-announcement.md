# Part 1 · The road ahead

The road ahead for Strategos (a working title): sixteen milestones, boiled down to six phases. **Now** is first in line: research and design are finished, building hasn't started, and there's nothing to play yet. **Next** is the road to the first playable version, a solo skirmish against the AI. **Later** is everything after it, starting with multiplayer. There are no dates: a phase is done when it passes its checks, not when a calendar says so. This is the current plan, and plans may change, more so the further out a phase is.

## Now · Take Command

*An army that goes where you point, and the controls to point with.*

- **Armies that find the way.** Hundreds of units cross hills, steps, bridges and fords on a single order, without getting stuck.
- **The commander's view.** A strategy camera that pans, zooms and rotates high above the field, in single player or on a server.
- **Orders you can read.** Box select, control groups, attack-move, and a preview of where every soldier will stand before you release the click.
- **Down among them.** Drop into first person, walk among your troops, tell them to follow you or to attack what you're looking at, then rise back to the sky.
- **The first battle.** Thirty spearmen against thirty, decided by the health bars.

## Next · The First Village

*Turn forest, rock and field into a town that rises block by block.*

- **Real trees, real rock.** Workers chop trees log by log and quarry rocky hillsides, with cracks, particles and sound as the land changes.
- **Four resources.** Food from berry bushes, fields, sheep and pigs, plus wood, stone and iron, each with something worth spending it on.
- **Built block by block.** Houses and barracks rise where you place them, even on a slope, shed blocks under attack and get them back on repair. Stray explosions and pistons can't touch them.
- **A first full match.** Gather, build, train and burn a scripted rival's civic centre, with rally points, an idle-worker key, a minimap and attacks announced in advance.
- **Save and resume.** Save mid-match and pick up exactly where you stopped.

## Next · Steel and Strategy

*Real armies and real counters, ending in the first playable version: a solo skirmish against the AI.*

- **Counters that matter.** Spearmen beat cavalry, cavalry beats archers, archers beat spearmen, and archers on high ground outrange those below.
- **Ring the bell.** Just as Minecraft's villagers hide when a bell rings, your workers take shelter in the civic centre, and each one inside adds an arrow to its volleys.
- **Village to Town.** Advance to Town, research upgrades you can see on your soldiers' gear, raise defence towers and roll out siege rams.
- **The first playable version.** A solo skirmish started from the title screen, against an AI with its own openings, three difficulty levels and a horn that sounds before it attacks.
- **Into the fog.** A fog of war to scout through: send riders ahead, uncover the map piece by piece, and never know for sure what waits behind the hill.

## Later · Friends in the Fog

*Two to four players on one server, and a fog of war that keeps everyone honest.*

- **Play together.** Take on your friends or team up with them against AI opponents, with a lobby, teams, and a way back in if your connection drops.
- **One faction, two commanders.** One of you commands from the sky while the other leads the troops on the ground in first person.
- **Fog that plays fair.** Allies share vision, enemy troops you can't see are never sent to your game, and enemy buildings stay as you last saw them until you scout again.
- **An AI that scouts.** Below the clearly labelled top level, AI opponents scout and remember what they've seen instead of peeking.
- **A first real look.** The first civilisation gets its proper art, replacing the placeholders.

## Later · Borders and Banners

*Claim land, take what you conquer, meet a second civilisation, and face an AI that plans ahead.*

- **Borders on the land.** Territory shows on the ground and on the minimap; you build only inside your own, and new civic centres claim more.
- **Capture, don't just raze.** Take enemy buildings, heal the wounded in garrisons, and hold chokepoints with towers, palisades and gates.
- **A second civilisation.** Its own units, bonuses, abilities and architecture, plus a third phase, City, and a full technology tree.
- **Veterans and nightfall.** Soldiers earn ranks, healers mend troops and armies march in formation. In the first Minecraft twist, night falls and the torches on your buildings push back the dark.
- **An AI that plans.** It expands, plans and announces its attacks, retreats when it's losing, and climbs five difficulty levels.

## Later · Beyond the Arena

*New maps, your own survival world, and a long list of extras, each decided one at a time.*

- **Fresh ground.** Random maps from a seed, four-player maps among them, tools to make your own, a full setup screen and a guided tutorial.
- **Your own world.** Play in an ordinary survival world: claim a valley, fight the AI there, and have survival friends drop by your town.
- **Expansions, chosen one by one.** Candidates include trade, heroes, catapults and stone walls, ships, wonders and relics, sacred sites and two more civilisations.
- **The long tail.** Candidates include scenarios and campaigns, replays, spectators, translations and accessibility options, data packs that add your own content, and original music.

# Part 2 · The announcement

## Strategos: Build It Block by Block, Command It From the Sky

*The full roadmap, from the first order to the open world.*

### A note from the developer

Hello, and welcome to Strategos.

I'm the solo developer behind Strategos (a working title), a real-time strategy mod designed for Minecraft: Java Edition 1.21.1 on NeoForge, with no other mods required. There's no studio and no team: I make the calls and play through every milestone, and an AI coding assistant writes the code and its tests. Research and design are finished and every phase is mapped out, but building hasn't started: there's nothing to download yet, and no dates in this post, on purpose. I'd rather show you the whole road now, potholes included, than surprise you later.

### The vision

Picture a valley seen from high above. You drag a box around your five workers and send them into the woods, and the forest really recedes, tree by tree, log by log. Stone comes out of a real hillside. A barracks rises block by block where you placed it. Across a shallow river, a rival town is doing the same, and when it comes for you, you'll hear the horn first. Then drop into first person and walk among the soldiers you trained.

Strategos is inspired by classic real-time strategy games such as 0 A.D. and Age of Empires II and IV.

Strategos is not affiliated with or endorsed by Wildfire Games, Microsoft or Mojang, and contains no code, data or art from those games.

### Five pillars

- **The land is the economy.** Forests recede, hills get quarried, fields get laid out and towns rise block by block, and you see and hear every change.
- **Clear from above, real from below.** From the strategy camera, every unit reads at a glance: whose it is, what it is, what it's doing and carrying, how hurt and how well equipped. First person shows the same world up close.
- **Your hands already know it.** Classic RTS habits work from your first match: box select, control groups, Shift-queued orders, an idle-worker key, rally points and order previews.
- **Few decisions, all real.** Boom or rush, which counter to build, when to take the high ground, ring the town bell or advance. Hundreds of simulated matches check that every choice can win.
- **An honest opponent.** The AI gives the same commands you do and announces its attacks. Only the top difficulty may get a helping hand, and it says so on the label.

### The road to the first playable version

Three rules shape it: arenas made for battle before ordinary survival worlds, solo before multiplayer, and a rough but complete match as early as possible. Even solo, every order goes through the server, so multiplayer grows from the same foundations instead of being bolted on.

**Take Command** (now) lays the bedrock: hundreds of units crossing real terrain on one order, the strategy camera and controls, first person, and a first battle of thirty spearmen against thirty.

**The First Village** (next) brings the economy: real trees and rock, fields, livestock and mines, buildings that crumble and get repaired, and saving mid-match. Its first crude full match against a scripted rival has one question to pass: would I play another?

**Steel and Strategy** (next) adds archers and cavalry, counters, high ground, the town bell, the step from Village to Town, siege rams and towers, and an AI with distinct openings.

That's where the first playable version lands, so let me be plain about it: a solo skirmish against an AI opponent, started from the title screen, aiming for matches of 20 to 35 minutes, on one map, with one civilisation on both sides, a fog of war to scout through, and placeholder art.

### What comes later

Multiplayer comes next. **Friends in the Fog** brings two to four players on one server, as rivals or as allies against AIs, co-op inside one faction, and a fog of war where enemy troops you can't see are never sent to your game. **Borders and Banners** adds territory, capture, palisades and towers, a second civilisation, the City phase, nightfall as the first Minecraft twist, and an AI that plans instead of following a script. Its top level is meant to beat me. **Beyond the Arena** looks further: random maps, a tutorial, matches in an ordinary survival world, and bigger ideas such as trade, heroes, ships and more civilisations, each decided on its own.

Plans may change. The further out a phase is, the rougher its plan, and playtests will reshuffle the later work.

### How it's being built

Everything gets automated tests as it's built, and AI opponents play hundreds of matches against each other to check the balance. The very first milestone measures how many units a server can afford, so your army's size is set by measurement, not wishful thinking. No milestone counts as done until it meets its performance targets, and none is accepted until I've played it.
